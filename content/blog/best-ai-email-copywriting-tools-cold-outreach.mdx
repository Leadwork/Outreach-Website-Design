AI can help organize a brief, generate variations, and edit wording. It cannot establish that a claim is true or that a prospect needs your service.

## Start with the job

For drafting and editing, evaluate general assistants such as [ChatGPT](https://chatgpt.com/) and [Claude](https://claude.ai/). For workflow or brand-writing needs, investigate [Copy.ai](https://www.copy.ai/) and [Jasper](https://www.jasper.ai/). For sales-writing feedback, review [Lavender](https://www.lavender.ai/).

These are evaluation options, not a tested ranking or a statement that one product universally outperforms another. Verify current features, supported integrations, plan prices, and data handling directly with each vendor. Do not choose solely from an old per-seat price.

## Build a useful brief

Provide the target audience, approved offer, tone, verified source material, and claims the writer may use. Include examples you have rights to share. Ask the tool to mark missing information rather than inventing it.

An effective instruction is to draft several concise approaches using only the supplied facts, with one clear next step. Keep client information out of tools unless its use is authorized and the product’s data handling is appropriate.

## Review before sending

Check every name, event, result, and relationship. Confirm that personalization reflects an actual source and that placeholders resolve correctly. Remove fake familiarity, unsupported guarantees, and claims copied from another business’s examples.

A writing score is a heuristic. It is not an inbox-placement test or proof that the copy will produce replies. Human review should assess relevance and accuracy, not merely maximize a score.

## Evaluate with real work

Use the same approved brief to compare tools. Record editing time, factual errors, usability, and cost. If testing campaign performance, hold targeting and other variables as steady as practical and define the outcome in advance.

The best choice is the tool that fits your process and governance while reducing useful work—not the one that produces the most copy.
