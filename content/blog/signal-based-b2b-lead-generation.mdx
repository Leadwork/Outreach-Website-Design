A business signal is an observable event that may make a conversation timely. Its value depends on the connection between the event and the problem you solve.

## Choose signals relevant to your offer

Useful categories to investigate include leadership changes, relevant hiring, funding, expansion, product launches, integration announcements, and explicit enquiries. Do not monitor every available event simply because a tool can collect it.

For each signal, write down the source, publication date, account, relevant role, and your hypothesis. A hiring announcement may suggest an operational change; it does not prove a budget or pain point.

## Verify before personalizing

Open the source and confirm the event concerns the right company and date. Distinguish announced plans from completed changes. Treat detected technology, company matching, and generated summaries as potentially incomplete.

An illustrative opener might be:

> I saw your current opening for a RevOps lead. Does that role also own [relevant workflow], or does another team handle it?

This asks a grounded question without pretending to know the company’s internal priorities.

## Route signals into an owned process

Assign research and follow-up responsibilities. Deduplicate accounts and check for existing conversations, customers, and opt-outs before outreach. Review signals on a cadence appropriate to the event; a product launch and a long hiring cycle do not have the same useful lifespan.

Keep stop rules synchronized across channels. A recent event does not justify continuing to contact someone who has declined.

## Test whether the signal helps

Compare accounts with similar fit and timing, using consistent reply and qualification definitions. Record research cost as well as outcomes. Avoid publishing universal reply lifts without a documented evaluation.

Start with one signal and one clear buyer problem. If the resulting conversations do not validate the hypothesis, revise the connection before expanding the monitoring stack.
