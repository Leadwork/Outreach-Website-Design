Low response can have several causes. Diagnose them separately instead of assuming a different subject line will solve every problem.

## 1. Check the audience

Confirm current roles and companies, remove duplicates, and compare records with written qualification criteria. Apply customer and opt-out exclusions across campaigns. Verify emails and flag uncertainty rather than treating every provider result as confirmed delivery.

## 2. Check the opening

Replace generic praise with a relevant, verifiable observation. Explain why it matters as a hypothesis, not as knowledge of the prospect’s private plans.

## 3. Check the value

Describe the problem and the specific work you do. Use documented proof where available. Remove invented familiarity, unsupported results, and vague claims such as helping every business grow.

## 4. Check the next step

Ask one clear question suited to the relationship. A resource offer may be appropriate for an introduction; a call can suit an explicit enquiry. Test rather than assuming one type of request always wins.

## 5. Check delivery

Inspect authentication, errors, complaints, and provider notices. Seed tests are samples, and open tracking can be distorted by automated image fetching. Separate accepted delivery from inbox placement and human engagement.

Be careful with the arithmetic: if all other assumptions are identical, increasing placement from 50% to 92% is a factor of 1.84, not four. If reply rates already use all sends as the denominator, applying a placement multiplier again can double-count the effect.

## 6. Check follow-ups

Each follow-up should add context or useful information. Stop automated steps on replies and opt-outs. Threading can preserve context, but it is not a guarantee of better delivery.

## 7. Check the offer

Make the audience, deliverables, price, term, and responsibilities understandable. Separate examples and targets from contractual commitments. Compare observed conversations with the intended buyer problem before changing the offer.

Change one important variable at a time where practical. Record cohort dates, sample sizes, and reply classification. A small sample or a short response window may not support a confident conclusion.
