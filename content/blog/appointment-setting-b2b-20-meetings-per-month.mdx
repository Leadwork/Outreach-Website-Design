An appointment is useful when the right person attends a relevant conversation. Define that outcome before asking a team or agency to book more meetings.

## Agree qualification criteria

Document company fit, role, relevant problem, and disqualifiers. Decide what information is required before booking and what can be explored on the call. Do not count every positive reply as a qualified opportunity.

## Give every reply an owner

Create simple categories: interested, information requested, later timing, wrong contact, and not interested. Assign coverage hours and response targets that the team can actually support. An urgent reply outside those hours needs a clear escalation path, not an implied round-the-clock service.

Answer information requests directly. If timing is the issue, record the agreed follow-up date. Respect a negative response and apply opt-outs across channels.

## Make scheduling easy

Provide a booking link and alternative times if needed. Confirm timezone, duration, attendees, and a short agenda. Send relevant reminders and make cancellation or rescheduling straightforward. Use additional channels only where appropriate permissions and context support them.

Before the meeting, give the salesperson the original conversation, company context, and qualification notes. Avoid making the prospect repeat information they already supplied.

## Model outcomes without guaranteeing them

An illustrative funnel might be 2,000 unique prospects, 4% replies, 25% positive replies, and 50% of positive replies booking: that produces 10 bookings before no-shows. These are assumptions, not Pro Lead Maker results or a forecast. Changing any assumption changes the output.

Measure attended meetings separately from bookings. Record why a booking was disqualified or missed and improve the process. Compare campaigns using the same definitions and observation window.
