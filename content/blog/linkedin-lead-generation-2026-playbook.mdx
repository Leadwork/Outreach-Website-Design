LinkedIn outreach works best when a recipient can quickly understand who you are, why you contacted them, and whether the conversation is relevant. A profile, prospect list, and message sequence should tell the same story.

## Start with the profile

Use a clear headline explaining the work you do and the people you help. Add genuine experience, a relevant featured resource, and evidence you have permission to share. Avoid meeting-count promises or client logos you cannot support.

Review targeting by company, role, geography, and likely problem. Sales Navigator may help with research, but buying a subscription does not establish permission to automate activity.

## Understand the account risk

[LinkedIn prohibits unauthorized third-party software that automates activity on its website](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1340567/automated-activity-on-linkedin?lang=en). A cloud tool, proxy, paid account, or conservative schedule does not make that activity compliant or eliminate restriction risk.

Use permitted workflows and respect account notices. Do not treat a published agency invitation count as an official safe allowance. Explain the actual workflow to the account owner before starting any managed activity.

## Make each message useful

An illustrative progression is a relevant introduction, a useful observation or resource, then a clear question if the conversation warrants it. This is not a required number of touches or a tested result claim.

If you send a voice note, make it specific and easy to understand. Do not imply you personally researched a prospect when you did not. Stop follow-ups on a negative response or opt-out, and coordinate with email activity to avoid duplicate pressure.

## Report people and activity separately

Track unique people approached, accepted connections, messages, replies, and qualified conversations. Follow-ups are additional activity, not additional unique prospects.

Sequence reply rate is unique respondents divided by unique prospects contacted. Do not add per-message percentages or infer a cumulative reply rate without knowing overlap and denominators. Compare like-for-like cohorts and report the dates and sample sizes.
