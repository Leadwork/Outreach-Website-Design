A sequence should give a recipient a reason to continue the conversation. Repeating the first email with more urgency adds pressure without adding value.

The sequences below are **illustrative**, not documented client campaigns. They have no claimed meeting totals or conversion lift. Adapt pacing to the audience and stop when a reply needs human handling or someone opts out.

## Sequence A: A new introduction

**First message:** explain why this person is relevant, describe one specific problem you help with, and ask a simple question.

> Hi [name], your team’s [verified public context] caught my attention. We help [audience] with [defined work]. Is [problem] relevant to you?

**Follow-up:** offer a genuinely useful resource or verified example related to the original question. Do not invent a case study.

**Close:** politely end the sequence. Do not imply a future appointment or relationship that does not exist.

## Sequence B: A business signal

Start with a verified event such as a relevant hiring announcement. Explain your hypothesis as a question rather than declaring the company’s internal problem.

> I saw the [role] opening. Does that team also handle [workflow], or does it sit elsewhere?

If the recipient engages, answer their question and offer a suitable next step. If there is no reply, one useful clarification may be more appropriate than a long automated sequence. A business event is not permission or proof of purchase intent.

## Sequence C: An explicit enquiry

When someone requests information, answer that request promptly within your stated coverage hours. Provide the relevant scope, cost, or explanation before asking for a call. Record any agreed next step in the CRM so another channel does not restart cold outreach.

## Measure the whole conversation

Count unique prospects, positive replies, qualified bookings, attended meetings, and opportunities separately. State the time period and denominators. A high reply count can include objections and opt-outs; it is not the same as sales interest.

For a real case study, retain the source campaign records, client permission, and limitations. Until those are available, present examples as examples. Clear expectations protect both the buyer and the delivery team.
