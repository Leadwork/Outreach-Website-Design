A useful outbound plan connects the audience, message, delivery setup, and reply process. Buying a sending tool alone does not provide that system.

## Define the audience and offer

Write a short ideal-customer profile: industry, company size, geography, relevant role, problem, and disqualifiers. Add a recent business event only when you can verify it and explain why it matters. A funding round or job change is context, not proof of budget or intent.

State what the prospect would receive and why it is relevant. Use documented evidence when describing past results; otherwise explain the process without a numerical outcome claim.

## Prepare delivery and data

Confirm your sending provider permits the intended use. Configure SPF and DKIM for the actual sender and review DMARC alignment. Google’s requirements vary by sender category; its bulk-sender guidance permits a DMARC policy of `p=none`. Follow [the official requirements](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/81126?hl=en), rather than treating an agency’s preferred configuration as a provider rule.

Deduplicate records, check role and company fit, verify addresses, and apply suppression lists before launch. Flag catch-all and uncertain results separately. Verification reduces uncertainty; it cannot guarantee delivery or permission to contact someone.

## Write and review the sequence

Start with a relevant observation, a concise explanation of value, and one clear next step. The appropriate number of follow-ups depends on the audience and context. Each should add useful information. Stop when someone opts out or when a reply requires human handling.

Approve copy before sending and check all resolved personalization fields. Never invent customer results, research, or familiarity.

## Route replies and measure learning

Assign an owner for positive replies, questions, objections, incorrect contacts, and opt-outs. Agree response hours and escalation rules. Define what makes a meeting qualified before booking it.

Review unique prospects contacted, positive replies, booked meetings, attended meetings, and opportunities. Keep examples separate from forecasts. Test one variable where practical, allow replies time to arrive, and avoid calling a winner from a fixed sample size without considering uncertainty.
