Infrastructure should support reliable, identifiable business communication. It cannot make unwanted messages wanted or guarantee inbox placement.

## Map the system first

Document the sending domains, mailbox provider, sending application, reply owner, and suppression process. Confirm that each provider permits your proposed use. For example, [Postmark requires permission-based subscriptions](https://postmarkapp.com/terms-of-service), so it should not be treated as a general cold-prospecting option.

Separate domains can reduce direct sending exposure for your main domain, but risk remains. Keep sender identity clear and provide a legitimate way to learn about your business. Avoid misleading domains or impersonation.

## Configure authentication from provider instructions

1. Set MX records using the values issued for your account.
2. Publish one SPF record covering authorized senders. Check the DNS lookup limit when adding includes.
3. Enable DKIM and verify signing on actual outgoing messages.
4. Configure DMARC reporting and check alignment for all legitimate sending systems before tightening enforcement.

Do not copy another company’s DKIM key or assume a generic Microsoft MX hostname fits your tenant. A DMARC monitoring policy and a rejection policy serve different operational purposes. [Google’s sender guidance](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/81126?hl=en) permits `p=none` for its bulk-sender requirement.

## Test the full workflow

Send a controlled message to accounts you own. Inspect authentication results, reply routing, links, and opt-out behavior. Seed tests can highlight problems but do not prove placement for every real recipient.

Check that a reply stops automated follow-ups and that a suppression decision applies across campaigns. Keep access under the client’s ownership, use individual permissions where possible, and document handover and renewal responsibilities.

## Plan a gradual launch

Begin with a relevant pilot list and a conservative operating cap agreed for the accounts. Official sending limits are ceilings, not recommended cold-email volumes. Review errors, complaints, and replies before increasing activity; there is no universal mailbox count or warmup duration that makes sending safe.
