No system can guarantee that every message avoids spam. Receiving providers make their own decisions, and a campaign can perform differently across audiences and mailbox services.

## Separate delivery concepts

A server accepting a message is not the same as inbox placement. Placement in a Promotions tab is also different from placement in Spam. Report these distinctions accurately rather than combining them into a single failure category.

Seed tests are diagnostic samples, not a census of your recipients. Open tracking is imperfect because privacy features and automated processing can load remote content. [Apple explains how Mail Privacy Protection works](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/data/en/mail-privacy-protection/).

## Check authentication and identity

Use the actual sending provider’s SPF, DKIM, and DMARC instructions. Inspect a delivered test message to confirm alignment. Make your sender identity and business contact path clear. Secondary domains reduce direct exposure of the main domain but do not eliminate brand or reputation risk.

Follow the relevant [Google sender requirements](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/81126?hl=en), including applicable unsubscribe requirements. Do not assume a plain-text opt-out sentence satisfies every provider requirement.

## Inspect data and relevance

Deduplicate contacts, verify current company/role, flag uncertain addresses, and apply suppression lists before each campaign. Stop follow-ups on a reply or opt-out. A message can be technically authenticated and still be unwanted.

Keep copy readable, relevant, and truthful. There is no universal list of words whose presence alone determines spam placement. Review misleading subjects, excessive links, broken personalization, and unsupported claims in context.

## Use a documented pause process

Assign someone to review delivery errors, complaints, restrictions, and reply quality. When a problem appears, pause the affected campaign or account, inspect the evidence, and record the corrective action. Resume gradually when the cause is understood.

Agree monitoring thresholds for the actual provider and account. Do not present internal operating preferences as published provider limits or promise that a set number of recovery days will restore delivery.
