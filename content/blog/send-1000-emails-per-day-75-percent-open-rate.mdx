Sending capacity is an operating constraint, not a promise that messages will reach an inbox or produce meetings. Before planning 1,000 emails a day, establish whether your target audience, offer, and provider permissions support that activity.

## Count messages, not just contacts

A sequence with several follow-ups consumes several sends per prospect. Replies, opt-outs, and suppression rules reduce the number actually sent. Forecast each day of the sequence rather than multiplying new contacts by working days.

For illustration, a planning cap of 30 campaign messages per mailbox would require 34 mailboxes to accommodate 1,000 messages. This is arithmetic, **not an official safe limit**, and adding mailboxes does not bypass provider policies or guarantee delivery. Account and tenant restrictions can apply across mailboxes.

## Authenticate before launch

Use your actual sending provider’s instructions for SPF, DKIM, and DMARC. Identify every legitimate sender before changing a DMARC enforcement policy. Google’s bulk-sender guidance permits `p=none`; a reject policy is not a universal prerequisite for sending. See [Google’s sender requirements](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/81126?hl=en).

Secondary domains can separate direct sending activity from your main domain. They do not eliminate brand, reputation, or delivery risk. Avoid deceptive sender names or subject lines that imply a previous conversation.

## Start with a pilot

Agree an initial audience, campaign cap, monitoring owner, and pause conditions. Increase volume only after reviewing delivery errors, complaints, replies, and available reputation data. A warmup-tool score cannot establish that real recipients want your messages.

If performance deteriorates, pause the affected activity and investigate authentication, list quality, relevance, and provider feedback. There is no fixed recovery period that guarantees restored reputation.

## Measure outcomes with defined denominators

Report unique prospects contacted, delivery errors, positive replies, qualified meetings, and attended meetings. Define whether a rate uses attempted sends, accepted messages, or unique people.

Do not use a 75% open rate as proof of inbox placement. [Apple Mail Privacy Protection](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/data/en/mail-privacy-protection/) can load remote content without a human opening the message. Treat opens as an imperfect diagnostic, and assess actual conversations separately.
