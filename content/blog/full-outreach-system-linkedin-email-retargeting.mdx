Multiple channels can provide useful context, but they can also repeat the same unwanted message. Coordination matters more than the number of tools in the stack.

## Start with one account plan

Use consistent qualification criteria and messaging across channels. Record which person owns each account and what has already happened. A reply, opt-out, or booked meeting should update the plan so another tool does not continue an obsolete sequence.

An illustrative progression might begin with permitted research, a relevant email introduction, and a LinkedIn conversation where appropriate. This is a planning example, not a sequence with a proven conversion lift.

## Review each channel’s rules

LinkedIn prohibits unauthorized automation on its website; conservative activity limits do not remove that restriction. See [LinkedIn’s guidance](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1340567/automated-activity-on-linkedin?lang=en).

Before using matched audiences or visitor-identification tools, establish what data you have, whether its use is permitted, and whether your audience meets the advertising platform’s requirements. A business-domain visit does not identify a particular decision-maker or prove intent to purchase.

## Scope retargeting separately

Retargeting requires creative, audience preparation, ad management, and media budget. It is not included in our standard email-and-LinkedIn package unless separately agreed. Do not estimate spend per prospect by assuming every uploaded record will match or receive an impression.

Start with a defined audience and budget, then review actual reach, frequency, and qualified outcomes. Additional impressions are not automatically additional value.

## Measure influence honestly

Record booking source separately from channels that may have influenced the conversation. Compare cohorts with similar audience and timing, and acknowledge differences. A prospect exposed to more channels may already have been more engaged, so observed lift alone does not establish causation.
