Enrichment adds context to a basic prospect record. The useful question is whether a field improves targeting or handling—not how many columns a file contains.

## Agree the schema first

Define required fields such as name, company, role, work email, and source date. Separate optional fields such as phone, funding, or detected technology. Specify how missing, conflicting, and uncertain values should be represented.

A claim of 15 fields should not imply that every field is populated for every record. Coverage varies by market, company size, provider, and the requested information.

## Use a controlled waterfall

A waterfall queries another source when an earlier source cannot supply an acceptable value. Record provider, timestamp, confidence, and lookup cost. Some providers charge for attempts or consume credits under different conditions; do not assume only successful results cost money.

Check conflicting values rather than automatically trusting the first one returned. A public profile can also be stale. Confirm important information close to the time it will be used.

## Preserve uncertainty

Technology detection and intent signals are often inferences. A job opening is not proof of budget, and an account-level research signal does not identify the individual buyer. Use these as context for a question rather than as facts about someone’s private plans.

Review AI-generated summaries against their sources. Do not allow a generated opener to invent a post, event, relationship, or result.

## Deliver a usable file

Deduplicate, normalize formats, verify email classifications, and apply exclusions. Include verification dates and risk flags. Review a representative sample against the agreed criteria and record issues before delivery.

Collect and use only appropriate data for the purpose and market. A vendor’s compliance label does not establish that every downstream use is permitted. Document sourcing and opt-out handling with advice appropriate to your circumstances.
